# Two-Minute Demonstration Video Script

**Target Duration**: 120 seconds (2:00)  
**Theme**: Inclusive, Multilingual, Nonpartisan Civic Tech for Nigeria's 2026/2027 Elections

---

### [0:00 - 0:25] The Problem & Mission
**Visual**: Screen shows confused voter messages circulating on WhatsApp with fake links (`linksnapro.com/cvrreg`) and conflicting interpretations of the new Electoral Act 2026.  
**Voiceover (Eng)**:  
*"With Nigeria's general elections approaching on January 16, 2027, millions of citizens face two huge hurdles: understanding their rights under the newly gazetted Electoral Act 2026 in their mother tongues, and avoiding dangerous phishing links impersonating INEC. Today, we introduce an independent, evidence-first civic tool built for Track 3 of the AI4Elections Hackathon."*

---

### [0:25 - 0:50] Multilingual Voter FAQ with Verified Citations
**Visual**: User selects Hausa, asks: *"Yaya ake tantance mai zabe da na'urar BVAS?"* (How is a voter accredited with BVAS?). The system retrieves Section 47 of the Electoral Act 2026 and outputs the answer in natural Hausa with certified civic vocabulary.  
**Voiceover (Eng / Hausa snippet)**:  
*"Watch as we ask in Hausa about voter accreditation. Unlike generic chatbots that hallucinate, our engine retrieves the exact gazetted clause—Section 47, Subsection 2—and provides the answer using certified Hausa civic terms, complete with a verbatim legal citation and date of retrieval. With one click, low-literacy voters can play crisp synthesized audio powered by Meta MMS-TTS."*

---

### [0:50 - 1:15] Currency Test (2026 Act vs 2022 Act) & Partisan Refusal
**Visual**: User asks: *"Can election results be transmitted electronically?"* followed by *"Which candidate should I vote for?"*  
**Voiceover (Eng)**:  
*"Election rules change. In our automated currency test, the engine strictly prioritizes the 2026 statutory guidelines over the repealed 2022 Act. And when baited with partisan questions like 'Who should I vote for?', the system immediately refuses, protecting democratic neutrality."*

---

### [1:15 - 1:45] Check Mode: Link & Message Verification
**Visual**: User pastes the deceptive URL `linksnapro.com/cvrreg`. The system instantly tags it as: *"Does not match any official source I can check (Warning: Known fraudulent registration clone)"*. Next, the user pastes `cvr.inecnigeria.org`—the system correctly confirms it *"Matches an official source"* without being tricked by its third-party CNAME host.  
**Voiceover (Eng)**:  
*"Now, Check Mode. A voter pastes a suspicious CVR registration link. Using our privacy-preserving local hash prefix engine, we detect the fake clone instantly without sending user data to third parties. Crucially, when testing cvr.inecnigeria.org, our engine recognizes official CNAME routing, preventing false alarms."*

---

### [1:45 - 2:00] Low Bandwidth, Open Source, and Ready
**Visual**: Screen displays mobile performance metrics (<30 KB total page weight, 2G network speed test) and the open-source GitHub repo under MIT License.  
**Voiceover (Eng)**:  
*"Ultra-lightweight under 30 kilobytes, zero personal data stored, verified across Hausa, Yoruba, and Igbo. Grounded in law, built for every citizen. Fully open source. Thank you."*
